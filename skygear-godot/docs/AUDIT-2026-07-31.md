# Audit — 2026-07-31

Scope: every document in `docs/` plus `DESIGN.md`, `STATUS.md`, `VFX-PLAN.md`;
the simulation (`game.gd`, `game_data.gd`, `cards.gd`, `workshop.gd`,
`deckwork.gd`, `coach.gd`, `player.gd` in part), the HUD (`hud.gd`, `ui.gd`,
`hud_layout.gd`), the harness (`tests/parity_test.gd`, run to completion:
**372/372 green**), `tools/text_audit.gd`, `tools/balance.gd`, `runlog.gd`,
`telemetry.gd`, `lanes.gd`, and the camera/captain paths of `view3d.gd`.

Where a claim below is dynamic rather than structural, I did not assert it from
reading — I ran a headless probe against the live simulation (script in the
session scratchpad, not committed). Probe outputs are quoted verbatim where
used. Findings are ranked by consequence. Each is marked **DEFECT** (the code,
the doc, or the check is wrong by the project's own stated rules) or
**JUDGEMENT** (a call the owner may reasonably decline).

---

## The headline

**The harness passes 372/372 while the second class does not implement its own
design.** Not a nuance of tuning — the one sentence the class is built on
("Head fills only where he plants himself") is false in the shipped
simulation, all three guards against the class's named failure state are
absent, and the checks that exist for both properties only test the directions
that pass. Every recurring failure mode the project has named — data with no
reader, two functions disagreeing about one number, a detector that cannot
fail, a claim asserted rather than measured — has a live instance inside this
one feature, and the documents that should have caught it say "BUILT AND
COMPLETE" at the top.

The rest of the game is in considerably better shape than that sentence
suggests, and section 4 says what to protect. But the audit's job is
consequence order, and this is the consequence order.

## 1. The Boilerwright — three defects, one root

### 1a. Head fills from close combat, exactly like Pressure — DEFECT

Design (`CLASS-2-DESIGN.md` §2, repeated in `game_data.gd:110-113` and again
at `game.gd:1830-1833`): *"his fills ONLY where he plants himself"*, and
*"standing still is the condition on all three"* taps.

Code: `register_damage` (`game.gd:1684-1695`) adds
`amount * 0.85 * pressure_rate` to `pressure` for **any** damage landed inside
210 units, with no `gauge_is_banked()` gate. `on_enemy_killed`
(`game.gd:1784-1786`) adds +9 per close kill, same. And `_fill_head`
(`game.gd:1859-1876`) checks position only — there is no stationary condition
at all.

Measured:

```
PROBE head_after_close_hit=68.00  (design says 0)   — one 40-damage hit
PROBE head_after_close_kill=9.85  (design says 0)
```

One close hit fills Head faster than eight seconds inside a Tap Main
(26/s). So in practice the taps, the vents and the Boiler are the *slow*
sources, and the class plays as a slower, tankier captain with a +45%
multiplier — which is precisely the "second question collapses into her
question" outcome that CLASS-2 §0 spends its whole opening refusing. The
sharpest ideas in the class (the commute, the geography of the three vents,
Bleed Jet as damage-not-done) are all priced against Head being scarce away
from prepared ground. It is not scarce; it is ambient.

Why the harness is green: the class block (`parity_test.gd:1740-2020`) tests
every **positive** fill — banked doesn't decay, Boiler fills it, tap fills
it — and never the negative: that fighting does *not* fill it. CLASS-2 §7's
own checklist names the missing check verbatim ("Head rises only on a tap and
only while stationary"); it was never written.

Fix shape (one-line semantics): gate the two grant sites on
`gauge_is_banked()`, then add the negative-space check. Decide `pressure_rate`
and the stationary condition at the same time — `HAIR TRIGGER` currently
amplifies the unintended fill, which is the only reason that "dead" card does
anything for him.

### 1b. The class's named failure state is live: free ship repair — DEFECT

CLASS-2 §2 names the failure — *"repairing the Boiler faster than three lanes
can break it — an objective you can top up indefinitely is unlosable"* — and
erects three guards. Today:

1. **The 0.6 HP/Head Boiler charge** — deliberately removed (documented at the
   top of CLASS-2, fine as a decision) — but it was guard #1, and the header's
   claim that "Blowdown's repair rate already encodes the loss" is arithmetic
   nonsense: 0.35 repair per Head is only a loss against a cost, and the cost
   is the thing that was removed. Boiler → Head is now free at 45/s.
2. **The 10 HP/s repair budget** (`heal_budget`-shaped) — never built. No
   `repair_budget` exists anywhere in `scripts/`.
3. **"No card may move either number"** — moot, since neither number exists.

Measured:

```
PROBE boiler_repaired=300.0 in 30s standing on the Boiler (cost to Boiler: 0)
```

Ten HP/s of sustained free objective repair, while also dealing Blowdown
damage into the lane-1 boarders converging on exactly that spot. Whether this
is *degenerate in play* depends on incoming Boiler DPS and is a measurement
(`tools/balance.gd` cannot currently make it — see 1d); whether the documented
guard exists is not a measurement, and it does not.

Worse, the check that claims to cover it is pointed at a phantom number.
`parity_test.gd:1843-1845`:

```gdscript
var spent: float = 100.0 * 0.6
_check("class", "and repairing the ship with the ship is a loss",
    gained < spent, ...)
```

`0.6` is the Boiler charge **that was deliberately not built**. The check
compares real repair (35) against an imaginary cost (60) and passes forever.
This is the "detector silenced to make a screen pass" failure mode in a new
costume: not silenced — aimed at a constant from a design the code rejected.
The comment block at `game_data.gd:208-213` still asserts the 42% loss "is the
whole guard against the failure this class could have had." Both the comment
and the check are wrong in the same direction, which is what makes this the
most dangerous kind of drift: an agent verifying the doc against the code
comments and the harness would find three witnesses agreeing on a falsehood.

### 1c. The dead captain cards are offered to him, and one breaks him — DEFECT

CLASS-2 §6: eight SCOPE_CAPTAIN cards are dead for him and "each card carries
a `can` predicate, so eight gain `and g.hero == "captain"`". Built: none.
`grep -rn 'hero\|class_id' scripts/cards.gd` returns nothing. Consequences,
in descending order:

- **SECOND WIND (`dashchg`, epic) gives the no-dash class three recharging
  dashes.** The `maxi(1, ...)` resync in `game.gd:671-675` only protects the
  zero-card case. Measured: `PROBE boilerwright_max_dashes_after_second_wind=3
  (design says 0 forever)`. "NO DASH AT ALL" is on his class-select card and
  his HUD; an epic in the draft revokes his defining constraint. The harness
  check "and nothing quietly gives it back" (`parity_test.gd:1760-1765`) tests
  only the cardless path.
- **BLOODSTEAM (lifesteal) works for him** through the same unguarded
  `register_damage` — CLASS-2 §2 says flatly "no lifesteal: she keeps herself
  alive, he keeps the ship alive."
- SPRING COILS, RAMMING GEAR, SAFETY VALVE, OVERPRESSURE-the-card are picks
  that do nothing (he never dashes from charges, never vents) — wasted card
  slots on a 3-card draft, which the design calls "a wasted card rather than a
  hard choice."
- The six new cards §6 specifies (LONG HOSE, BLEED VALVE, SECOND MAIN,
  REGULATOR, CROSS-BRACE, SUPERHEAT) are absent. Absent is a fine decision —
  but it is not in the doc's deliberately-not-built list, so the next agent
  cannot tell a cut from an oversight.

### 1d. The class shipped without its own instrument — JUDGEMENT, but note it

CLASS-2 §8 is unusually good: it names the real risk (the commute), designs
the cheapest possible test, and specifies the metric ("seconds with Head at
zero and no boarder within 400 units; above 10% of wave time the geography is
wrong"). Neither exists: `telemetry.gd` has no Head field, and
`tools/balance.gd` plays the captain only. The class was built past its own
§8 without the number §8 said to collect first. Given 1a, the metric would
currently measure the wrong game anyway — but when 1a is fixed, build the
metric in the same commit, because that is the moment the commute question
becomes real.

**On the status header:** CLASS-2 opens "Status: BUILT AND COMPLETE," lists
two deliberate omissions, and says "where the build disagrees, the build is
the newer decision and the difference is commented at the code." Sections
1a-1c mean the header is wrong three ways, the omissions list is missing at
least four entries (stationary condition, the close-combat guard, the repair
budget, the card gates), and the code comments assert the design rather than
the behaviour. This matters beyond the class: the departures-list pattern is
the project's whole defence against doc drift, and this is the case study in
what happens when the list is incomplete — every downstream reader (me
included, until the probe) starts from "complete" and audits for tuning
instead of for existence.

## 2. The meta layer — the claims vs the checks

### 2a. "The whole tree is worth less than three cards" — the check does not test the claim — DEFECT (in the check) — RESOLVED SG-11 (2026-08-01)

**Resolution:** the check was rewritten (first by an earlier pass to sum the
offensive fields, then by SG-11 to measure the card side from the live catalogue
too). It now reads the maxed tree's offensive product (crit_chance+range+
vent_radius = **×1.31**) against the three per-skill upgrade cards resolved from
`cards.gd` (**×2.28**), and `shop · the three-card yardstick is the real
catalogue, not three typed numbers` fails the build if the card side is ever typed
back in. Measured in full, the inequality HOLDS. The finding below stands as the
record of the vacuous version it replaced.

`META-PROGRESSION-DESIGN.md` §4.1 (and `workshop.gd`'s header): *"a check
compares resolved stats: full tree without cards, against no tree with a
typical wave-6 card set."* What `parity_test.gd:1476-1491` actually does:

```gdscript
var tree_damage: float = 1.0 + float(everything.get("crit_chance", 0.0))
var three_cards := 1.30 * 1.35 * 1.30
_check(..., tree_damage < three_cards, ...)
```

The whole tree is reduced to its crit chance (×1.06) against ×2.28. Max HP
+24, +8% move, +10% range, +180 Boiler, wave heals, cannon buffs — none of it
is in the comparison. The check can only fail if a single node's crit exceeds
+60% per rank. The claim may well be TRUE — the tree is genuinely flat and
additive, and the no-multiplier check beside it is real — but the doc says
"a check asserts it rather than trusting me," and what is asserted is a
strawman. `SHIP-AND-MAPS-DESIGN.md` §7.2 then re-describes this check as
"parity_test.gd already runs `plain` against `kitted` on seed NOSHOP to prove
the full Workshop is worth less than three cards" — which is a different
check (`shop · and a bought tree actually changes the run`) proving the
opposite-direction property (the tree does *something*). The claim has now
drifted through three documents, gaining strength each time. Recommendation:
implement the check the doc describes — resolve a kitted run and a three-card
run into damage-per-second against a fixture boarder, and compare those.

### 2b. Counting drift: 23 ≠ 24 nodes, 7 ≠ 11 sigils, 5 ≠ 7 sources — DEFECT (documentation)

- `SkyGearWorkshop.NODES` contains **23** nodes (kit 7, gauge 5, ship 6,
  log 5). META doc header: "24 Workshop nodes." The missing one is **Second
  Breath** (gauge, +12% move after a vent) — in the design table, absent from
  the code, absent from the departures list. Undocumented drop.
- Sigils: the doc's §2 lists eleven sigil sources (Quick Passage, No Second
  Thoughts, Unshaken, Full Spectrum, Monochrome…). `firsts_in`
  (`workshop.gd:384-403`) implements a **different seven**: first_win,
  win_boilerwright, reach_eight, won_close, won_unhealed, heat_1, heat_2.
  Not one of the doc's named sigils beyond the win/heat ones exists under its
  documented trigger (Unshaken is "healed under 150" in the doc, "healed <= 0"
  in code, under a different name). The economy holds — 11 sigils to buy
  seven Articles against 7 obtainable — but nobody can compute that from any
  single source.
- `workshop.gd:151` comments "Sixteen sigils to own the seven" — sixteen is
  the cost of the *designed nine*; the built seven cost eleven.
- `parity_test.gd:1682` hardcodes `var sigil_sources := 5  ## the firsts in
  firsts_in` — the actual count is 7. The check passes anyway.

Four numbers, four places, no two agreeing. This is "two functions
disagreeing about one number" migrated into the meta-economy, and the fix is
the same as it was for `rail()`: one function. `firsts_in` already *is* the
truth — derive the harness's count from `ARTICLES`/`firsts_in`, and strike
the numbers from prose (see section 5's house standard).

### 2c. The meta doc's own build order was skipped — JUDGEMENT

§8 of the meta doc is explicit: run the scrip formula over the sixty logged
runs first, then ship the system with **no tree UI** and hand-edited specs for
ten runs, then the smallest screen that works. The commits show the full
Workshop screen shipping in the same day as the system. The doc's reasoning
for the staging ("the front door is worse than the game") was not refuted, it
was bypassed. The screen exists now and unbuilding it would be silly — but
the §8.1 measurement (scrip curve over the real `runs.json`) still costs one
script and would still settle the earn rate against reality instead of the
four-row projection in §2. Cheap, still worth doing, still not done.

## 3. Coherence and quality of the game itself

### 3a. Is it one game? Mostly, yes — and the boundaries are the achievement — JUDGEMENT

The layer discipline is real and unusually well-defended: the draft moves
rates and multipliers in-run; the Workshop moves starting values, flat and
capped, gated behind a first win; the Articles are verbs; Heat is named
modifiers on one pinned baseline; deckwork's only currency is time; fittings
(designed, unbuilt) may only touch the stowage and verb tables. Each boundary
has at least one enforcing check, and two boundaries (no-multiplier talents,
every-field-has-a-reader) are enforced mechanically. This composes into one
game rather than several because every layer answers a different question at
a different timescale. That is rare, it was done on purpose, and it should be
named as the thing the project must not lose.

Where composition genuinely fails today is the second class (section 1): as
shipped he is the captain with a different body and a free multiplier, so the
game currently contains one and a half classes, not two.

Smaller frictions, none fatal:

- **Three teaching surfaces, all captain-only.** HOW TO PLAY teaches her loop
  ("your gauge fills from damage you land within 210 units… at full it VENTS
  by itself") — every sentence false-by-design for the Boilerwright, and the
  page does not say so. The coach's four lines are hers; two ("gauge is
  full — it vents by itself"; "the gauge only fills inside 210 units") are
  wrong for him and will fire at him — holding a full bank is his *correct*
  play and the coach calls it a mistake, twice a run, in the strip the Boiler
  warnings use. The `CLASSES[...].compare` rows — written to answer exactly
  this — still have no reader (`OUTSTANDING.md` owes the screen). Until
  that screen exists, the coach should at minimum gate `gauge` and `kiting`
  on `gauge_is_banked()`: four lines of the cheapest kind.
- **The auto-attack and Overpressure**: `_process_basic_attack` applies the
  ×1.45 but never calls `spend_overpressure()`; casts do. So Scald is
  permanently boosted for free while the bank holds. Possibly intended
  (an auto costing bank would drain him passively) — but it is documented
  nowhere, and CLASS-2 §2 says "at 10 Head a cast" of "every drafted skill",
  which is exactly the loophole. Decide and write it down.
- **Blowdown's cannon repair caps at the wrong number**: `game.gd:2094` clamps
  to `SkyGearLanes.TURRET.hp` (the const, 760) rather than `turret.max_hp`,
  which Shot Locker raises. One number, two owners — the exact class of bug
  the project has had three times. Today it only means a kitted cannon can't
  be over-repaired, which is benign; the pattern is not.
- **"41 cards" is 40.** `cards.gd`'s header, `DESIGN.md` §13 and CLASS-2 §6
  all say forty-one; `catalogue()` appends forty (`grep -c cards.append` =
  40). Either a card was lost in the port or the browser count included the
  weapon card. Nobody can currently tell which, which is the problem.
- **`choose_draft`'s legacy kinds** ("damage", "health", "boiler",
  "pressure", "dash" at `game.gd:1020-1030`) have no producer anywhere —
  dead branches from Milestone 1. Data with no *writer* this time. Delete
  them; they are exactly the kind of thing a future agent will assume is
  reachable and preserve.

### 3b. Where the real decisions are — JUDGEMENT

Genuinely good, in order:

1. **The draft is the game, and the card faces earn it.** Before→after
   computed by running the real `apply` against a sandbox (`cards.gd:210`),
   leak-checked and rng-checked by the harness; element-owns-hue,
   rarity-as-metal, role-as-word, each with a check. This is the
   best-executed system in the project and the doc claim "the draft decides
   the run" is structurally true — protect it, and protect the
   one-axis-per-channel rule when the next agent wants a fifth channel.
2. **The close-range loop** is a real decision (proximity buys sustain and
   tempo against risk), it is instrumented (`close_share` is the project's
   own KPI), and its failure mode (range-kiting) has three named guards that
   all exist — unlike the Boilerwright's three.
3. **The events** change what a wave asks rather than how much: the blackout
   (information removed, payment offered) is the standout and the design
   note in `game_data.gd:39-54` — named, readable, pays out — is a house
   rule worth generalizing.
4. **Deckwork's time-only currency** is sharp: cost measured in what three
   lanes did while you stood still. But it still has **no on-screen prompt**
   (`OUTSTANDING.md` owes it), and an interaction nobody is told about is an
   interaction nobody performs — as shipped it is a system-shaped secret.
5. **Heat** does exactly what it claims: one baseline until a win, every
   harness claim pinned to rung 0, rungs named not numbered.

The illusion-of-decision column is short: the Workshop's stat nodes are
deliberately boring (the doc says so and chose it), and the Log branch —
information that widens the draft — is where the actual decisions live;
if the tree ever grows, grow that branch. The Boilerwright's "where will
the fight happen" is currently an illusion, per section 1.

### 3c. The economy of restarting — one interaction worth watching — JUDGEMENT

`restart_run` keeps the seed (correct: "the only version of a restart that
lets you learn anything"), but `scrip_for` quarters a repeated seed. So the
player who uses restart the way the restart comment recommends — replay the
same twelve waves until you crack them — is the player the economy pays
least. That is defensible (anti-farm) and possibly even intended, but the two
comments argue different values and neither mentions the other. One sentence
in the meta doc would settle it.

## 4. The UI, at a high level

The bottom-band HUD with the objective alone at top is coherent, and the
enforcement stack behind it — `hud_plates` as the single geometry source, the
six-size layout matrix, the F4 editor editing the real panels, `SkyGearInk`
as the single text funnel, the text audit's containment + collision +
contrast passes — is the strongest UI-tooling story I have seen in a project
this size. Screens agree with each other because they are made to by
machinery, not by care.

Gaps, in order:

1. **The game does not teach its second class anywhere** (3a). This is the
   largest player-facing UI gap and it is already on `OUTSTANDING.md`; the
   coach actively mis-teaching him is not on it and should be.
2. **HOW TO PLAY contradicts its own comment.** The header says "read from
   the same tables the simulation reads, so this page cannot drift" — and the
   Boiler HP in the body is a literal `500` (`hud.gd:1835`), the player HP is
   `SkyGearPlayer.MAX_HP` (wrong for one of two classes), and the harness
   check `howto · the close range it teaches is the one the game uses`
   asserts only that three constants are greater than zero — a check that
   cannot fail. Same shape as 1b: the safeguard exists in prose, not in fact.
3. **The `framed` exemption in the widget audit.** `text_audit.gd:176-181`
   skips plate-containment for any widget declared while no plate was open
   (`framed` defaults false in `ui.gd:117`). Screens that draw widgets
   without `_panel`/`_stamp` — the title is one — opt out silently. That is
   the same trapdoor the pause-menu bug crawled through, one level up. Cheap
   hardening: the audit reports the unframed count per screen, so a screen
   going from 9 framed to 0 is visible.
4. The harness truncation canary (`EXPECTED_AT_LEAST := 250` at
   `parity_test.gd:121`) is a third stale against the actual 372 — a pass
   could shed 120 checks before it trips. It exists because a silent
   truncation actually happened; keep it honest with a number near reality,
   or better, derive it (e.g. fail if `checks` drops below 95% of a committed
   count file the harness updates on green).

The pause loadout column, the run report as the results screen, the
`run_logged` honesty line, edge markers testing feet rather than heads, the
countdown numbers on cooldowns — all small, all correct, all evidence that
somebody keeps asking "what question does the player have right now."

## 5. The documentation, judged as instructions for agents

Per document, could a competent coding agent build the right thing from it?

- **`OUTSTANDING.md` — the best file in the repo.** The rule (nothing leaves
  except done-or-dropped-with-reason), the provenance rule (nothing enters
  from self-generated ideas), and entries that carry their own diagnosis
  quality (the popup-drift entry — "left open rather than closed, because 'I
  could not reproduce it' is not the same" — is the model). Two corrections:
  the Done list contains "**The second class — the Boilerwright, complete**",
  which section 1 falsifies; and the deckwork entry should absorb the
  coach/class-teaching gap. Move the class from Done back to Open with the
  four specific absences named.
- **`STATUS.md` — stale enough to mislead.** "161 checks" (now 372), "last
  updated 2026-07-28", a "Next up" list that predates the Workshop, the
  Articles, Heat, deckwork, and the second class. It bills itself "read this
  first after a break" — an agent obeying that starts three days behind, in a
  repo where three days is ten systems. Either update it in the same commits
  that change what it summarizes, or delete it and let `OUTSTANDING.md` +
  `git log` be the entry point (my recommendation: delete; two read-me-firsts
  that disagree cost more than one that's missing).
- **`DESIGN.md` — an accretion log wearing a spec's title.** §§1-10 describe
  Milestone 1: "top-down in Milestone 1", "draft implemented, simplified
  cards", "push waves not yet ported", "40 checks". The truth lives in
  §§13b-13m, appended chronologically. An agent reading top-down builds a
  game that stopped existing on the 27th; the file is only honest read
  bottom-up, and nothing says so. Cheapest fix: a one-paragraph banner at the
  top — "sections 1-10 are historical; current truth is §13x and
  OUTSTANDING.md" — until someone has a day to rewrite it.
- **`CLASS-2-DESIGN.md`** — the strongest *reasoning* document (the rejected
  alternatives in §0, the named failure state, §8's cheap-test-first
  discipline are exactly what a future agent needs to not re-litigate). And
  the wrongest *status* document — see section 1. The pattern is right; the
  omissions list is incomplete, which converts the whole header from a map
  into a trap.
- **`META-PROGRESSION-DESIGN.md`** — the best departures list in the repo
  (seven explicit build-vs-design differences with reasons: this is the house
  pattern). Marred by the counting drift (2b) and by describing the §4.1
  check as stronger than it is (2a).
- **`SHIP-AND-MAPS-DESIGN.md`** — very good, and §10 ("Where I am guessing")
  should be mandatory in every design doc. Already stale twice on its own
  publication day: §2.3's "two of three lanes can never damage the Boiler"
  was confirmed and **fixed** by commit `e32210d` (the `MERGE_FROM`
  convergence in `correct_enemy_position`), so the doc's "most important
  three lines" now describe superseded code — and its consequence #1 ("no
  object can funnel a boarder") is still true but its consequence #3 is not;
  and §5's "existing bug: `record` writes … and not `heat`" is fixed
  (`game.gd:973`). Neither fix is noted in the doc. An agent implementing
  stowage from this doc inherits two false premises.
- **`VFX-PLAN.md` / `VFX-RESEARCH-AUDIT.md`** — good; the DONE section
  pattern with dates works, and DESIGN §13m recording the audit response
  ("a critique read and not acted on is worse than one that never arrived")
  is the right ritual. The remaining items (§3 ribbons, §4 fog, §6 weapon
  trail, §5 chromatic) are correctly tracked in OUTSTANDING.
- **`docs/HUD-LAYOUT.md`** — does its one job; matches the code.

### The house standard I would set

The project's documented defence against drift is the departures list, and
section 1 shows its failure mode: the list is only as good as its
completeness, and nothing checks completeness. Four rules would close most of
what this audit found:

1. **Three exhaustive lists per design doc**: BUILT / DELIBERATELY NOT BUILT
   (with the reason) / NOT YET BUILT. The rule that makes it work: every
   numbered claim in the doc's own body must appear in exactly one list.
   "BUILT AND COMPLETE" as a freestanding sentence is banned — completeness
   is the property the lists prove, not a word the header asserts.
2. **No counts in prose.** "372 checks", "24 nodes", "41 cards", "eleven
   sigils" have all drifted. Cite the command (`godot --headless --script
   tests/parity_test.gd`) or the table (`SkyGearWorkshop.NODES`); if a number
   must appear, it appears once, in code, and the doc points at it.
3. **A doc claim of harness coverage names the check string.** "Covered by
   the harness" cost this project the Boilerwright: the coverage was
   one-directional and nobody could tell. "Covered by `class · standing
   nowhere loses nothing`" is greppable, falsifiable, and makes a claim with
   no named check visibly untested. This is the documentation-side twin of
   the every-field-has-a-reader guard, and it is the single highest-leverage
   rule on this list *for coding agents specifically*.
4. **Acting on a doc's finding updates the doc's line, same commit.** The
   lane-merge case shows same-day drift; the convention already exists for
   OUTSTANDING.md and just needs extending to the design docs' findings.

## 7. What to protect

Said plainly, because it is as load-bearing as the defect list: the layer
boundaries and their mechanical enforcement (3a); the draft and its
computed-not-described card faces; the close-range loop and `close_share` as
the project's KPI; `OUTSTANDING.md`'s rules; the one-geometry-source /
one-text-funnel HUD architecture and its audit stack; the tools-first
standing instruction, which produced `parity.py`, the F4 editor, the text
audit and the ingest pipeline — four tools that have each already caught
something a person had asserted wrongly. The commit-message discipline is
also worth naming: half the project's real reasoning lives there, and it is
consistently honest about its own failures, which is why this audit could be
specific.

## 8. The single first change

Gate the two Head grant sites on `gauge_is_banked()` (`register_damage`,
`on_enemy_killed`) and land, in the same commit, the negative-space check
CLASS-2 §7 already specifies: *close damage far from any tap grants zero Head*.
Everything else in section 1 becomes visible and decidable once that is true —
the repair loop gets a real cost (Head becomes scarce), the commute metric
becomes worth building, the dead cards become obviously dead, and the class
starts asking the question it was designed to ask. It is a two-line simulation
change plus one check, and it converts the largest false claim in the project
("BUILT AND COMPLETE") into a merely incomplete one.
