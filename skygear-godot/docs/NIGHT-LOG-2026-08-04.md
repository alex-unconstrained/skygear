## The rig fault this pair found, and it is SG-190 one frame deeper

SG-190 established that a hand-stepping rig must be inside a physics frame or
`move_and_slide()` integrates the idle wall clock. What it did not establish is
that being inside a physics frame is enough.

Measured in `tools/crew_probe.gd`, after `Engine.physics_ticks_per_second = 20`
and two `await physics_frame`s:

| | |
|---|---|
| `Engine.physics_ticks_per_second` | 20 |
| `Engine.is_in_physics_frame()` | true |
| `root.get_physics_process_delta_time()` | **0.01667** |
| what `move_and_slide` actually integrated | **0.05** |

The walk identity caught it: `ground / (|v| × DT)` came back **2.87**, and
0.05 / 0.01667 is 3.00. A rig that asks for a clock and is told it has one is not
a rig that has one.

`crew_probe` sets no tick rate at all — it takes the rate the engine is already
running at, steps at exactly that, and proves the identity above every result.
Three times as many steps per run, and worth it.

The same identity was added to `melee_probe`, where it reads **1.00** in every
arm the subject actually walks in — so that rig's 0.05 step is sound and its
numbers stand. It reads 0.33 when the subject is pressed against the captain's
collider, which is `move_and_slide` sliding along a body and not a clock fault at
all; the sample is restricted to frames with room to walk for exactly that
reason. That near-miss is worth remembering: **0.33 is what a real clock fault
looks like, and it is also what standing next to something looks like.**
